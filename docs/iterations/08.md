## Native durable journal

The append-only journal stores full work data, exact root seed, pinned finite
limit, connection/generation provenance, consumed attempt indices and outcomes.
Each transition has a bounded chained checksum and is flushed before the host
may perform its next dependent action. The loader validates legal transitions
as well as byte integrity. A lifetime OS lock prevents two cooperative clients
from using the same journal simultaneously.

The host consumes an index durably before preparation/computation, records a
send intent before invoking the socket submission, and persists an exact ACK
before claiming acceptance or rejection. On restart, unfinished computation or
an unsent proof becomes `interrupted`; a pending send becomes `uncertain`.
Neither is automatically retried. All terminal outcomes remain recorded.

The policy deliberately allows a skipped attempt when the exact crash boundary
cannot prove more. Later cumulative row state is still reproduced from the
original seed and index. It does not pretend to provide exactly-once delivery
over a socket after an acknowledgement is lost.

Recovery needs a fresh authorization and notify. The coordinator can restore a
consumed prefix only into a fresh, unused generation. Old verified proof objects
cannot cross that boundary. A/B/A work changes retain A's prior progress. A
different header or target has its own identity even when the textual job ID
is the same.

Review found and fixed an encoding-only duplicate risk: gzip negotiation used
to participate in the proposed persistent search key. A gzip-to-plain change
could otherwise restart attempt zero after an uncertain send. Encoding now
remains exact transport provenance while the consumed search prefix is shared
across encoding variants. A dedicated real restart scenario verifies this.

Storage failures are fatal and stay outside transport retry handling. Corrupt,
torn, empty-existing or mismatched journals are preserved and refused, never
silently reset. Report paths and temporary paths that alias the journal are
rejected before opening it or connecting. See `docs/search-recovery.md` for
the API, ordering and finite capacity limits.
